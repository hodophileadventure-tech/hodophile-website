# Kashmir — Arang Kel (Arangkel) & Neelum Valley — 5 Days

Overview
- Region: Neelum Valley, Azad Kashmir (Arang Kel near Kel)
- Difficulty: Moderate (road travel + short steep hikes to Arang Kel)
- Best time: May–October (winter access limited by snow)
- Typical start: Muzaffarabad (or Islamabad with a drive to Muzaffarabad)

Day 1 — Muzaffarabad Arrival & Transfer toward Keran/Leepa
- Morning: Arrive Muzaffarabad. Brief city orientation—markets, cash exchange, and supplies.
- Midday: Drive north into Neelum Valley toward Keran or Keran/Kel area depending on road conditions (drive times vary; plan several hours with stops at scenic pullouts).
- Afternoon: Check into a guesthouse or riverside accommodation in the chosen valley town (Keran/Arang Kel approach base). Meet local guide to confirm Arang Kel access options (chairlift/trek depending on season).
- Evening: Dinner and rest; review next day’s early start.

Day 2 — Reach Kel & Hike to Arang Kel
- Early morning: Drive to Kel (local jeep/vehicle may be required for last stretches). Arrange porters/guide if needed.
- Mid-morning: From Kel, hike up to Arang Kel (commonly a steep 45–90 minute hike depending on fitness and route). Alternate: short chairlift/ropeway options exist in some seasons—confirm locally.
- Midday: Arrive Arang Kel—settle into the meadow; panoramic views over Neelum River and terraced slopes.
- Afternoon: Explore Arang Kel (easy walks around meadow, photography, meet locals). Picnic or packed lunch.
- Evening: Stay overnight in basic hillside guesthouse or camp (many visitors sleep in Kel and return after sunset; if camping, prepare warm gear).

Day 3 — Explore Nearby Valleys & Villages
- Morning: Descend to Kel for breakfast, then drive further up Neelum Valley (toward Keran, Sharda area, or to lesser-known hamlets depending on road permit and season).
- Midday: Short hikes to river viewpoints, visit local villages, try Kashmiri cuisine at village dhabas.
- Afternoon: Visit waterfalls or small side valleys; photography and cultural interactions.
- Evening: Return to base town for overnight.

Day 4 — Day Trip to Keran / Taobat (depending on permissions)
- Option: If permits and road conditions allow, take a longer day trip toward Keran or Taobat (closer to Line of Control areas—check security updates and travel advisories).
- Activities: Scenic drives, river beaches, local markets, and short walks.
- Note: Border-area travel can be sensitive—always travel with local guide and check with authorities.

Day 5 — Return to Muzaffarabad / Departure
- Morning: Pack and depart back toward Muzaffarabad.
- Midday: Stop en route at viewpoints and souvenir stalls; lunch in a valley town.
- Afternoon: Arrival Muzaffarabad; trip ends.

Logistics & Practical Notes
- Permits & Security: Parts of Neelum Valley are near the Line of Control; check current security advisories, carry ID/passport, and register with local authorities or hotel where required. Some areas may require police permits—confirm in Muzaffarabad.
- Transport: Use reliable 4x4 or local jeeps for last-mile access. Hire trusted local guides/drivers.
- Accommodation: Simple guesthouses in Kel or riverside towns; camping in Arang Kel is possible in warm months.
- Safety: Avoid night travel in remote stretches. River banks may be steep; supervise children near water.
- Packing: Warm layers for nights, waterproof jacket, headlamp, sturdy hiking shoes, snacks, sunscreen, insect repellent, first-aid, cash (many small businesses prefer cash).
- Estimated budget (per person): Transport & jeep PKR 8,000–25,000 depending on vehicle and route; Accommodation PKR 2,000–8,000/night; Meals PKR 500–1,500/day.
- Cultural notes: Respect local customs; ask before photographing people or private property.
